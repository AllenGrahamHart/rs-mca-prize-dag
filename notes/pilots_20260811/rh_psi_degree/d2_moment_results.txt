==============================================================================
  dbar = (rho+2)rho(rho-1)/(N(N-1));  T_1 = 2, T_2 = rho, O = 0, def = 0
  S2reg = a*m + a(a-1)*dbar - 2rho^2        (type-2 second moment)
  CS    = (a*m - 2rho)^2 / rho              (Cauchy-Schwarz equality)

  LIMIT: dbar -> m/4, S2reg -> a^2 m/4, CS -> a^2 m/4, ratio -> 1.
  So at the regular pair degree the second moment sits EXACTLY on the
  Cauchy-Schwarz equality case: variance 0, all X_gamma equal, max = mean.
  At finite m the regular value sits BELOW CS, so the only thing the second
  moment forces is that W-internal pair degrees EXCEED the global mean --
  a LOWER bound on max, useless for an upper bound.
  A second-moment instrument therefore has to beat the regular pair degree
  by a positive amount before it says anything at all about the maximum.

  And no moment bound can ever reach the target: max >= mean always, while
  the target need_X = mean_X - (4m - def_in + o_g + o_h)/rho < mean_X.
  Chebyshev/Cauchy-Schwarz give max <= mean + sqrt(V(T_2-1)) >= mean.

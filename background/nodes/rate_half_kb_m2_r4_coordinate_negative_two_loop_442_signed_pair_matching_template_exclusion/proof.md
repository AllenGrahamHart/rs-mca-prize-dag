# Proof

For forced type `cD`, removing the forced product leaves

```text
a=cE, q=sigma DE, x=DF, y=EF.
```

For forced type `sigma DE`, it leaves

```text
a=cD, q=cE, x=DF, y=EF.
```

These are exactly `(KB44T-1)`.  Complete-fiber product injectivity makes
the six entries nonzero and distinct.

Write the product-pair row as

```text
r(u,v)=[uv,-(u+v),-1].                           (1)
```

Suppose `a,q` form one involution pair.  The other four entries must be
matched in one of the three ways `(KB44T-2)`.

For the internal matching, the two rows are

```text
r(x,-x)=[-x^2,0,-1],
r(y,-y)=[-y^2,0,-1].                             (2)
```

Since `x^2!=y^2`, their common kernel is generated by `(0,1,0)`.  Hence the
bilinear involution equation is `u+v=0`: product negation.  But one of the
two common pairs in `(KB44O-2)` is `(-1,c)`, `(b,c)`, or `(c,-1)` according
to the label family.  Negation would force respectively `c=1`, `b^2=c^2`,
or `c=1`, all excluded by `(KB4P-2)`.

For the first cross matching, subtract the equations for `(x,y)` and
`(-x,-y)`:

```text
2 Alpha(x+y)=0.                                  (3)
```

Distinctness gives `x+y!=0`, so `Alpha=0`.  The second cross matching
similarly gives `2 Alpha(x-y)=0`, and `x-y!=0`.  Thus either cross matching
requires `Alpha=0`.

The exact cross products in the outside-product compiler give, after
discarding only `b,c` and harmless signs, the factors in `(KB44T-3)`.
Eliminating `b` and then `l` against each row ideal gives the printed norms.
Their prime divisors lie in `{3,5,7}`, whereas the deployed characteristic
is congruent to `2,3,2` modulo `3,5,7`.  Hence `Alpha!=0` in every row.

There are six common rows, two values of `sigma`, and two affected forced
types.  Each loses three of its fifteen residual matchings, proving the
count `72` and the residual cap `468`. QED.

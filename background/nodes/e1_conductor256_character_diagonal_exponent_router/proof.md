# Proof

The quotient group

```text
G=(Z/256Z)^x/{+-1}
```

has order `64` and is cyclic, generated by the class of `5`.  The odd
integers `1,3,...,127` are one representative system.  Write `g_t=5^t` in
`G`, with indices modulo `64`.

For `a,b in G`, the conductor-256 circular unit formula gives

```text
log |eta_a(zeta^b)|^2
 =2 log |sin(pi*a*b/256)/sin(pi*b/256)|
 =f(ab)-f(b),                                          (1)
```

where `f(b)=2 log |sin(pi*b/256)|`.  The root-of-unity factor has modulus
one.  Hence `(CER2)` and `(CER3)` give

```text
lambda_b(u)
 =sum_(a!=1) x_a(f(ab)-f(b))
 =sum_(a in G) xi(a)f(ab).                             (2)
```

In the cyclic coordinates `a=g_t,b=g_s`, equation (2) is the convolution in
the first line of `(CER4)`.

Use the unnormalized Fourier transform

```text
widehat(h)_j=sum_(t=0)^63 h(g_t) exp(-2 pi i j t/64).  (3)
```

Changing variables from `s` to `s+t` in the transform of (2) yields

```text
widehat(lambda)_j
 =widehat(f)_j widehat(xi)_(-j)
 =kappa_j widehat(xi)_(-j),                            (4)
```

which proves `(CER4)`.

The full-unit basis theorem says that the 63 columns

```text
log |eta_a(zeta^b)|^2,       a!=1,
```

are linearly independent.  Equivalently, (2) is injective on the
63-dimensional complexification of the zero-sum functions on `G`.  The
characters diagonalize (2).  Its eigenvalues on the nontrivial character
lines are exactly `kappa_j`, so injectivity proves `(CER5)`.  This step is
also why no unproved numerical lower bound for a sine logarithm is being
used.

For two collisions in one fixed cofactor, the common-prime associate router
proves `(CER6)` and

```text
sum_s |lambda_(g_s)(u)|<=R.                            (5)
```

Therefore every Fourier coefficient of `lambda` has modulus at most `R`.
Equation (4) and `(CER5)` give `(CER7)`.

Parseval for the convention (3) says

```text
sum_t |xi(g_t)|^2=(1/64)sum_j |widehat(xi)_j|^2.       (6)
```

The trivial coefficient vanishes because `xi` has sum zero.  Inserting
`(CER7)` into (6) proves `(CER8)`.  Fourier inversion and the triangle
inequality give

```text
|xi(g_t)|
 <=(1/64)sum_(j=1)^63 |widehat(xi)_j|
 <=(R/64)sum_(j=1)^63 |kappa_j|^(-1),                 (7)
```

which is `(CER9)`.  Finally, Parseval applied to `lambda`, followed by
`||lambda||_2<=||lambda||_1`, gives

```text
sum_j |kappa_j|^2 |widehat(xi)_(-j)|^2
 =sum_j |widehat(lambda)_j|^2
 =64 sum_s |lambda_(g_s)|^2
 <=64R^2,                                              (8)
```

proving `(CER10)`.

The right sides of `(CER8)` and `(CER9)` are finite because there are only
63 nonzero explicit eigenvalues.  Thus only finitely many integer exponent
vectors can occur.  The circular-unit representation is unique modulo
`mu_256`, so no associate orbit is lost.  Applying the exact coefficient and
sparse-product filters from the dependency is necessary and sufficient to
retain the profile vectors inside each enumerated associate family.  This
proves the stated finite-search reduction. QED.

# Proof

Work on the reduced core-stripped curve

```text
C:Qbar=0,       bideg Qbar=(d,e),       d=rho-1.
```

Let `s_F` be the nonzero contact section supplied by the contracted
recurrence. Its line bundle is

```text
L_F=O_C(-rho-1,e+1).                                  (1)
```

The pole-cancellation ideal for the residual-domain-locator ratio has
colength `p`. Since

```text
5(b+1)>p,
```

there is a nonzero biform `F` of bidegree `(4,b)` that clears those poles.
It gives a regular section `s_G=FG/H`. Four contact copies produce

```text
s_F^4 s_G in H^0(C,O_C(-1,b+ell+3)).                  (2)
```

Indeed, the two surface degrees are

```text
(N-1)+4-4(rho+1)=-1,
b-(4e+1-ell)+4(e+1)=b+ell+3.                          (3)
```

Under `(QCE2)`, the restriction sequence is

```text
0 -> O(-d-1,b+ell+3-e)
  -> O(-1,b+ell+3)
  -> O_C(-1,b+ell+3) -> 0.                            (4)
```

The middle surface bundle has no sections. Both coordinates of the left
bundle are negative, so its `H^1` is zero by Kunneth. Consequently the
space in `(2)` is zero.

We now treat containment rather than assuming it away. Factor `Qbar` over
the algebraic closure. Call a component active when `s_F` is nonzero on it,
and let `Q_A` be the squarefree product of all active components. There is at
least one because `s_F` is nonzero.

If `F` failed to contain an active component, then `s_G` would also be
nonzero there: a mixed component is contained in neither the domain locator
nor the slope locator. The product in `(2)` would be nonzero on that
component, contradicting `(4)`. Hence every active component divides `F`,
so

```text
r_A=deg_X Q_A<=4.                                     (5)
```

Every other component is contact-inactive. Reducedness of `C` gives

```text
Qbar=Q_A Q_0,       deg_X Q_0=d-r_A,
N_F=Q_0 K,          deg_X K<=r_A-1,                   (6)
```

where `N_F` is the polynomial numerator representing the contact section.
The second identity follows because a zero restriction on every other
irreducible component means divisibility by their squarefree product.

Use reversed domain coordinates in the full recurrence identity. With the
same names for the reversed factors, it reads

```text
Q_A Q_0 Y=N_F+u^(d+rho)R
          =Q_0 K+u^(d+rho)R.                          (7)
```

There is no domain-only factor in `Qbar`, so `gcd(Q_0,u)=1`. Equation `(7)`
forces `Q_0` to divide `R`, and cancellation gives

```text
Q_AY=K+u^(d+rho)R_1.                                  (8)
```

Because `deg_u K<r_A<=4`, the coefficients of `Q_AY` in degrees
`d,...,d+rho-1` vanish. Thus the coefficient vector of `Q_A`, padded to
length `d+1`, is a nonzero rational right-kernel vector of the contracted
Hankel pencil. That pencil has generic rank `d` and `d+1` columns, so its
rational kernel is one-dimensional and is generated by the primitive
degree-`d` vector `Qbar`. Since `d>4>=r_A`, the carrier vector cannot be
proportional to `Qbar`. This contradiction proves `(QCE2)`.

Negating the strict inequality gives

```text
floor(p/5)>=e-ell-3.
```

Since `p<=Delta`, every survivor obeys `(QCE3)`.

For the official row, use

```text
p<=Delta=4m-1-2e,       ell<=4e-4m-1.                 (10)
```

If `e<=floor(16m/13)-1`, then

```text
floor(Delta/5)+ell+3
 <=floor((4m-1-2e)/5)+4e-4m+2<e.                     (11)
```

The final strict inequality follows already from `13e<=16m-13`. For
`m=2^37`, the next integer is the value in `(QCE5)`, completing the proof.
QED.

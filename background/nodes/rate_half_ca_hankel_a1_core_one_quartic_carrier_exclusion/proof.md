# Proof

Work on the reduced core-stripped curve

```text
C:Qbar=0,       bideg Qbar=(d,e),       d=rho-1.
```

Let `s_F` be the nonzero contact section supplied by the contracted
recurrence. Its line bundle is

```text
L_F=O_C(-rho-1,e+1).                                  (1)
```

The pole-cancellation ideal for the residual-domain-locator ratio has
colength `p`. Since

```text
5(b+1)>p,
```

there is a nonzero biform `F` of bidegree `(4,b)` that clears those poles.
It gives a regular section `s_G=FG/H`. Four contact copies produce

```text
s_F^4 s_G in H^0(C,O_C(-1,b+ell+3)).                  (2)
```

Indeed, the two surface degrees are

```text
(N-1)+4-4(rho+1)=-1,
b-(4e+1-ell)+4(e+1)=b+ell+3.                          (3)
```

Under `(QCE3)`, the restriction sequence is

```text
0 -> O(-d-1,b+ell+3-e)
  -> O(-1,b+ell+3)
  -> O_C(-1,b+ell+3) -> 0.                            (4)
```

The middle surface bundle has no sections. Both coordinates of the left
bundle are negative, so its `H^1` is zero by Kunneth. Consequently the
space in `(2)` is zero.

We now treat containment rather than assuming it away. Factor `Qbar` over
the algebraic closure and choose an irreducible component `C_i` on which
`s_F` is nonzero. Write its bidegree as `(r_i,e_i)`. The component is mixed,
so both degrees are positive, and nonvanishing of `(1)` gives

```text
(e+1)r_i-(rho+1)e_i>=0.                               (5)
```

If `F` did not contain `C_i`, then `s_G` would also be nonzero on `C_i`:
the mixed component is contained in neither the domain locator nor the
slope locator. The product in `(2)` would then be nonzero on `C_i`,
contradicting `(4)`. Hence `F` contains `C_i`, and `r_i<=4`.

The core-one range has `e<=floor((rho-1)/2)`. If `e_i>=2`, then

```text
(e+1)r_i-(rho+1)e_i
 <=4(e+1)-2(rho+1)<0.                                 (6)
```

If `e_i=1` and `r_i<=3`, `(QCE2)` makes the same degree negative. Therefore
every contact-active component contained in `F` has bidegree exactly
`(4,1)`. Since `F` has domain degree four, there is exactly one such
component; call its equation `Q_4`. Every other component is
contact-inactive. Reducedness of `C` now gives

```text
Qbar=Q_4 Q_0,       bideg Q_0=(d-4,e-1),
N_F=Q_0 K,          bideg K<=(3,2),                   (7)
```

where `N_F` is the polynomial numerator representing the contact section.
The second identity follows because a zero restriction on every other
irreducible component means divisibility by their squarefree product.

Use reversed domain coordinates in the full recurrence identity. With the
same names for the reversed factors, it reads

```text
Q_4 Q_0 Y=N_F+u^(d+rho)R
          =Q_0 K+u^(d+rho)R.                          (8)
```

There is no domain-only factor in `Qbar`, so `gcd(Q_0,u)=1`. Equation `(8)`
forces `Q_0` to divide `R`, and cancellation gives

```text
Q_4Y=K+u^(d+rho)R_1.                                  (9)
```

Because `deg_u K<=3`, the coefficients of `Q_4Y` in degrees
`d,...,d+rho-1` vanish. Thus the coefficient vector of `Q_4`, padded to
length `d+1`, is a nonzero rational right-kernel vector of the contracted
Hankel pencil. That pencil has generic rank `d` and `d+1` columns, so its
rational kernel is one-dimensional and is generated by the primitive
degree-`d` vector `Qbar`. Since `d>4`, the degree-four vector cannot be
proportional to `Qbar`. This contradiction proves `(QCE3)`.

For the official row, use

```text
p<=Delta=4m-1-2e,       ell<=4e-4m-1.                 (10)
```

If `e<=floor(16m/13)-1`, then

```text
floor(Delta/5)+ell+3
 <=floor((4m-1-2e)/5)+4e-4m+2<e.                     (11)
```

The final strict inequality follows already from `13e<=16m-13`. Condition
`(QCE2)` also holds throughout this range. For `m=2^37`, the next integer is
the value in `(QCE5)`, completing the proof. QED.

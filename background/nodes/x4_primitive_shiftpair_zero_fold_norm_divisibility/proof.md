# Proof

For every dyadic order `n_j`, reduction of `C(X)` modulo `X^n_j-1` and then
modulo `Phi_n_j=X^(n_j/2)+1` gives exactly the coefficient vector `beta_j`.
Consequently

```text
beta_a=0  iff  Phi_n_a(X) divides C(X) in Z[X].     (1)
```

The reverse implication is immediate.  For the forward implication,
`C(zeta_n_a)=0`; cyclotomic irreducibility gives divisibility in `Q[X]`, and
the monicity of `Phi_n_a` plus Gauss's lemma gives divisibility in `Z[X]`.

Also `C(1)=|P|-|Q|=0`, so `X-1` divides `C`.  The polynomials `X-1` and the
distinct `Phi_n_a`, `a in Z`, are pairwise coprime monic polynomials.  Their
product therefore divides `C` in `Z[X]`.

Fix `j in S` and write `O=n_j`.  The standard dyadic norm identities are

```text
|Norm_(Q(zeta_O)/Q)(zeta_O-1)|=2,                  (2)

|Norm_(Q(zeta_O)/Q)(Phi_M(zeta_O))|
   =2^(min(O,M)/2)                                  (3)
```

for distinct dyadic `O,M>=4`.  For completeness, if `M>O`, then
`Phi_M(zeta_O)=2`, whose norm is `2^(O/2)`.  If `M<O`, the element lies in
the subfield generated by a primitive root of order `2O/M`; its norm there
is two and the extension degree is `M/2`, giving `2^(M/2)`.

Evaluating the integral factorization of `C` at `zeta_O`, and using (2)--(3),
shows

```text
2^(1+sum_(a in Z)min(n_j,n_a)/2)
  divides |Norm(beta_j)|.                           (4)
```

Multiply (4) over `j in S`.  The dyadic domain has odd characteristic, so
this power of two is coprime to the proved divisor `p^R_S`.  Their product
therefore divides the norm product, proving `(ZF-1)`.  The Haar product upper
bound

```text
product_(j in S)|Norm(beta_j)| <=(eN/A_S)^A_S
```

then gives `(ZF-2)` after multiplication by `A_S^A_S`. QED.

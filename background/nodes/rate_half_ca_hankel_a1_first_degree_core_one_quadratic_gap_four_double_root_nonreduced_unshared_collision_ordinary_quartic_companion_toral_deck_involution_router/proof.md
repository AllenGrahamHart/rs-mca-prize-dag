# Proof

The shape and row-saturation dependencies give four distinct roots of
`Q(-,x)` in `Gamma` for every classified row `x in U_0`. We first check
absolute irreducibility. The factor is irreducible over `F_P(X)`. If it had
two Frobenius-conjugate geometric components, both would have bidegree
`(2,3)` and intersection number twelve, while all `4|U_0|>12` rational grid
points would lie in their intersection. Four conjugate components are
impossible because their equal row degrees would sum to six. Hence `Q` is
absolutely irreducible.

The resultant in `(QDI2)` is divisible by `(X-Y)^4`: over the generic
splitting field, each of the four roots of `Q(t,X)` contributes one factor
vanishing at `Y=X`. A quartic resultant is homogeneous of degree four in
each coefficient packet, whose row degrees are at most six. Its raw
bidegree is at most `(24,24)`, so

```text
bideg L_Q<=(20,20).                                 (1)
```

It is nonzero by the same fixed-root argument as in the quadratic router;
otherwise absolute irreducibility would fail.

There are `4|U_0|=18e-14` grid incidences. A slope has at most six distinct
rows, so the total defect from the capacity `6|Gamma|=18e` is fourteen.
At least `F_6=3e-14` slopes therefore have six distinct rows. Each gives
thirty ordered off-diagonal pairs. A fixed pair comes from at most four
slopes because its two row specializations are nonzero quartics. Thus the
number of distinct pairs is at least

```text
ceil(30F_6/4)=P_6.                                  (2)
```

For a connected separable degree-six cover, geometric off-diagonal
fiber-product components correspond to point-stabilizer orbits on the five
other sheets. There are at most five. Suppose none of their row-coordinate
images is a translated subtorus. Every image has bidegree at most `(20,20)`.
On its normalization the Euler-characteristic estimate is

```text
chi<=2*20*20=800.                                  (3)
```

The Corvaja--Zannier theorem then bounds each component by the maximum of a
cube-root term and a characteristic term. Both are strictly below `P_6/5`,
as certified by

```text
125*17280000*N^2 < P_6^3,
5*4800*N^2 < 2^167 P_6.                            (4)
```

The union of at most five components would contain fewer than `P_6` points,
contradicting `(2)`. Hence one component image `Z` is a translated subtorus

```text
X^aY^b=k,       gcd(a,b)=1.                        (5)
```

Moreover, at least one such toral component contains a counted point of
`H^2`: otherwise the same five-component sum over the non-toral components
would still contradict `(2)`. Choose `Z` with that property.

Let `W` be the corresponding normalized fiber-product component, let `h`
be the degree of either projection `W -> C`, and let `q` be the degree
`W -> Z`. Since the row coordinate `X:C -> P^1` has degree four, the two
coordinate degrees on `W` give

```text
q|a|=q|b|=4h.                                     (6)
```

Primitivity forces `|a|=|b|=1`. Thus `(5)` assigns a unique second row,
`Y=kX` or `Y=k/X`, to a generic first row. The six row values in a generic
fiber are distinct, so `h=1`. Therefore `W` is the graph of a nonidentity
deck automorphism `sigma` of `C -> P^1_t`.

In the scaling case, one grid point gives `k in H`. The order of `sigma`
equals the order of `k`, divides the cover degree six, and also divides
`|H|=2^41`. It is nontrivial, so it is two and `k=-1`. In the reciprocal
case, applying `X |-> k/X` twice fixes both generators `t,X` of the function
field, so `sigma` is again an involution. This proves `(QDI4)`.

The fixed field of `X |-> -X` is generated by `W=X^2`; the fixed field of
`X |-> k/X` is generated by `W=X+k/X`. The degree-six polynomial in `X`
therefore descends to a degree-three polynomial in `W`. Its parameter
degree remains at most four, yielding the two forms in `(QDI5)`. QED.

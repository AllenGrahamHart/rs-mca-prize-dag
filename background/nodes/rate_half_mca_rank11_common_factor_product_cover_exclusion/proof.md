# Proof

Polynomial multiplication is bilinear. Therefore the span of all products
`pb`, with `p in P` and `b in B`, is generated by at most
`dim(P) dim(B)<=6` products of basis elements. Under `(PC1)`,

```text
sum_i U_i <= sum_i W_i <= span(PB),
```

which proves `(PC2)`. The low-span residual payment says an unsafe line has
`dim(sum_i U_i)>=7`, so the product-cover branch is impossible there.

For `(PC3)`, restrict to the anchor-good coordinate universe `G`, where
`|G|<=m`. For `x in G`, all polynomials in `W_i=g_iB` vanish at `x` exactly
when either `g_i(x)=0` or every member of `B` vanishes at `x`. Let

```text
C={x in G: every b in B vanishes at x}
  union {x in G: every p in P vanishes at x}.
```

If `x` is outside `C`, evaluation on the two-dimensional pencil `P` is a
nonzero linear functional. Its kernel is one projective point, so at most one
of the projectively distinct `g_i` vanishes at `x`. Hence a coordinate outside
`C` belongs to at most one selected zero set `J_i`, while a coordinate inside
`C` belongs to at most all `b` of them. Double-counting incidences gives

```text
bH <= sum_i |J_i| <= b|C|+(|G|-|C|)
    <= m+(b-1)|C|.
```

Rearranging and taking the ceiling proves `(PC3)`. Exact substitution at the
official terminal yields

```text
ceil((8406*42452-1116048)/8405)=42325.
```

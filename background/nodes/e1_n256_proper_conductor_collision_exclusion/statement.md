# E1 N=256 proper-conductor collision exclusion

- **status:** PROVED
- **closure:** proof plus exact arithmetic

Let `zeta` be a primitive `256`-th root and let

```text
alpha=F(zeta)=sum_(i=0)^127 c_i zeta^i
```

have either first-surviving `N=256,s=5` folded profile
`(a,b,c)=(4,2,0)` or `(3,4,0)`. Let `T={i:c_i!=0}` be the actual folded
support and, for any `r in T`, put

```text
d=gcd(256,{i-r:i in T}).
```

If `d>1`, then `alpha` cannot vanish modulo any pair-feasible row prime.
Consequently every unresolved collision in the first `N=256` band has full
conductor:

```text
gcd(256,{i-r:i in T})=1.
```

Indeed, after a monomial normalization a proper-conductor vector belongs to
the cyclotomic subfield generated by `eta=zeta^d`, of degree at most `64`.
Its small-field norm is a nonzero integer of magnitude at most
`18^32<2^250`. The full norm is a power of that same integer up to sign, so
it has no prime divisor at or above `2^250`.

This is only a conductor reduction. Full-conductor vectors do occur in the
low-variance `(3,4,0)` residual; in particular a certified example has
variance `36`. The theorem does not close either first-band profile.

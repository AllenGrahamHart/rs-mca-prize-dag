# Proof

Let `D_x0:F^(n+1)->F^n` be the row-difference map

```text
(c_0,...,c_n) |-> (c_1-x_0c_0,...,c_n-x_0c_(n-1)).    (1)
```

Then `L(t)=D_x0 M(t)` and

```text
ker D_x0=span{(1,x_0,...,x_0^n)^T}.                   (2)
```

Over `F(t)`, the full pencil has rank `rho=n-1`. Its two-dimensional left
kernel is generated by the two shifts of `q(t)`. Pairing the vector in `(2)`
with those shifts gives

```text
Q(t;x_0),       x_0Q(t;x_0).                          (3)
```

The clean fibre factorization `Q(t;x_0)=A_0(t)S(t)` is nonzero. Thus for
generic `t` the vector `(2)` is not in the image of `M(t)`: an image vector
is orthogonal to the whole left kernel, while `(3)` is not. The restriction
of `D_x0` to `im M(t)` is therefore injective, proving

```text
rank L(t)=rank M(t)=rho.                               (4)
```

The matrix `L(t)` is symmetric. A symmetric `n x n` matrix of rank `n-1`
has rank-one adjugate, and its right and left radicals are both generated by
`q(t)`. Hence over `F(t)`

```text
adj L(t)=D(t)q(t)q(t)^T.                               (5)
```

Primitivity of `q` makes the gcd of all products `q_iq_j` equal to one.
Since the left side of `(5)` is polynomial, the scalar `D` is polynomial.
Every adjugate entry has degree at most `n-1=4m-1`, so

```text
deg D<=4m-1-2m=2m-1.                                  (6)
```

The generic parameter normalization may additionally put parameter infinity
outside the finite marked rank-drop set. Then `L_1` has rank `n-1`, its
adjugate is nonzero, and the top coefficient of `q` is nonzero. Comparing
the degree-`n-1` coefficient in `(5)` makes `(6)` an equality. This proves
`(MAS2)`. Because `q` specializes nontrivially at every projective parameter,
`adj L` vanishes exactly when the rank drops below `n-1`, proving the root
statement.

We next identify the scalar. The Kronecker decomposition of the full
rectangular pencil has one right `L_m` block, two left `L_m^T` blocks, and a
regular block of size `m-1`. Let `Delta(t)` be the determinant of the regular
block. The generic coordinate choice makes its degree exactly `m-1`.

The rank-`n-1` compound of `M` has rank one. With

```text
u_0=(q_0,...,q_rho,0)^T,
u_1=(0,q_0,...,q_rho)^T,                              (7)
```

its right Pluecker factor is `q`, its left Pluecker factor is
`star(u_0 wedge u_1)`, and its scalar factor is `Delta`. Apply the
`(n-1)`st compound of the constant row map `D_x0`. Direct contraction of
`(7)` with the kernel vector `(1,x_0,...,x_0^n)` gives

```text
Q(t;x_0)q(t)                                           (8)
```

as the resulting left factor, up to a nonzero constant. Consequently

```text
adj L(t)=c Delta(t)Q(t;x_0)q(t)q(t)^T.                 (9)
```

Comparison with `(5)` proves `(MAS3)`, including multiplicities. Its degrees
also recover `deg D=(m-1)+m=2m-1`.

The moment representation gives the diagonal factorization in `(MAS4)`.
The `(i,j)` entry of the adjugate is the signed determinant obtained by
deleting row `j` and column `i`. Apply Cauchy-Binet to its factorization
through `R_U` and the diagonal matrix. Only `rho=n-1` element subsets occur,
and this gives `(MAS5)`. When `i=j=rho`, the retained row exponents are
`0,...,rho-1`, so both factors are ordinary nonzero Vandermonde
determinants. For other cofactors the generalized minors are retained with
their actual values and may vanish.

Finally `Q(t;x_0)=A_0(t)S(t)`. The degree-`m-1` squarefree factor `A_0`
has exactly the supported slopes incident to `x_0` as its roots. If `S` is
supported, it repeats one of those roots; otherwise it adds no supported
root. Thus `Q(t;x_0)` has exactly `m-1` distinct supported roots. The factor
`Delta` has at most `m-1` distinct supported roots. The clean endpoint makes
every remaining supported `q(gamma)` squarefree and `D`-split. This proves
`(MAS6)` and the final assertion. QED.
